# Stars Evidence: the wanted stars in GTA VI

Compiled 2026-11-12 (seven days before release). Second research pass, one subject: the wanted stars. Every row records something opened and read in this session. Nothing is from memory, earlier games, leaks, fan wikis or search-result summaries. I did not view any footage or images; where a row depends on what the Extended Look shows, the lead's own frame examination is the check.

Standing context: Rockstar's July 2026 sessions at Rockstar North were hands-off. Rob Nelson (Head of Development and Co-Studio Head, Rockstar North) held the controller. The build was a July build.

Individual star levels, in short:

- **One star**: minor; easy to escape by leaving the area (Nelson, twice).
- **About two stars**: still quite escapable (Nelson to Famitsu, in translation).
- **Three stars, in the city**: hard to escape by vehicle unless it is very fast or a sports bike; go on foot (Nelson).
- **Three or four stars**: a full shootout with several police cars on Vice Beach in the demo (TGG's recollection, "I believe").
- **Four stars**: the level shown in the Extended Look chase (TGG, Bailey, reading the footage).
- **Five stars**: nothing said by anyone.
- **Six stars**: the display has six positions (IGN, Bailey). Map-wide search is hearsay ("I was told", TGG). No one reports seeing six stars lit.
- **Thresholds**: unpublished (IGN: too early to know; Nelson: stars are not given out easily).

## (c) Conflicts between sources, and against the manual

1. **Name of the unidentified state.** Nelson: hollow white stars. GR-GUIDE: black and white stars. Same thing; the developer's term wins. The manual already uses "hollow".
2. **Who is the source for solid, flashing and red.** The manual's two pages cite GTABase and VICE for these readings ("Guides and creators read...", "Creators and one guide read..."). Both are relays. The origin is **TGG**, who was in the room, in his video of 29 August and his notes on X. Citing him directly is both more accurate and stronger: the claim becomes "one attendee's account" instead of "guides". GTABase names no source; VICE names TGG's post.
3. **Gray stars versus the attendee's model.** The manual's new page records gray stars at about 11:48-11:52 and 12:08 with five icons. TGG uses what appears to be the same clip (TGG-VID 1:47-1:57: CCTV, couple, outfits, appearance, and one more indicator) to illustrate Heat and **says nothing about the stars' colour there**. His four-state model has no gray. Two readings are possible and neither is established: (i) gray is a fifth state, as Wilson guesses; (ii) the gray frames are the dim phase of TGG's "flashing white" state, which a still frame cannot distinguish. **This is my own untested suggestion, not a sourced claim.** The lead can test it: step through consecutive frames around 11:48-11:52 and 12:08 and see whether the stars alternate between gray and white. Until then the manual should not say that flashing "cannot be shown" and gray "is a distinct state" as if those were independent facts.
4. **Is flashing new?** GR-GUIDE says changing star appearance is a series first. TGG says the flashing state is "just like previous GTA". The manual no longer makes the series-first claim on the pages I read; keep it out.
5. **The hollow-star scene.** TGG (and VICE, relaying him) describe the Extended Look's hollow-star clip as Lucia leaving a bank she has presumably robbed and riding past police alone. The manual's frame note at about 18:04-18:11 describes two masked characters getting onto a motorcycle across the road from a jewellers, with a jewelry-store interior just before. Either these are different clips or one description is wrong. The lead's frames are the better evidence; do not import TGG's "bank" or "alarm" wording into the manual without checking. Nelson's own hollow-star example is different again (a stolen car, in the private demo).
6. **Red stars: two phrasings.** The manual's page presents TGG's reading (escaped, still in the search area) and Wilson's (evaded, police still hold information) as two readings. They are not really in conflict: TGG's notes say both things in one sentence. Bailey's "presumably... still hunting you but not in active pursuit" is a third, compatible, independent reading that the manual does not cite and could.
7. **Splitting up.** Nelson: lowers the police's perception; takes some heat off. TGG: can drop a star. GTABase: reduces by one star (flat, unsourced). Use Nelson's wording; report the star as one attendee's claim.
8. **Six stars and the map-wide search.** TGG's video says six stars; his X notes say "the highest wanted level". Both are "I was told", speaker unnamed.
9. **What "solid" means.** TGG: direct line of sight. GTABase's recap: an identified target being pursued. These can come apart (identified but out of sight). Only TGG's version has a first-hand origin.
10. **Manual, "The Wanted Level" page, first paragraph.** It labels the six positions "demonstrated" but cites only the GamesRadar+ guide. Now that the lead has the frames, cite the Extended Look itself; IGN-VID 1:40 is the best written support.
11. **Manual, "The Stars Change Appearance", white-stars note.** "Rockstar has not said so in the sources read" is correct. It could add that the reading originates with a creator who attended the preview.
12. **Manual, "Two white stars also appear later... 18:24 to 18:44".** If these follow the hollow stars at 18:04-18:11 in the same sequence, that would be a hollow-to-solid change with icons appearing, which no source read describes. Worth a note in the unknown list rather than an interpretation.

Nothing found contradicts the manual's Nelson paragraphs (one star not a big deal; hollow white stars; escalation is the player's choice).
